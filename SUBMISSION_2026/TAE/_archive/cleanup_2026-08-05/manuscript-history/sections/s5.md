## 5 Why it moves — the mechanism <!-- page budget: 1.75 pg incl. Table 2 and the Figure 2 caption -->

The shift in the conditioning variable is composition of world states, not a change in what the agent
says about a given world. The world the agent is reporting on — shortage tier, reservoir elevation,
drought index — is written by the simulator, independently of the generation being governed, so it
can be held fixed after the fact. Standardising both arms on a common distribution of that world
state removes the shift. Against a raw gap in HIGH-appraisal prevalence of
{{CLAIM:anchor_high_delta}}, holding shortage tier fixed leaves a residual of {{CLAIM:world_residual_coarse}}
across {{CLAIM:world_coverage_coarse}} of the anchor corpus, and the finest stratification the data
supports leaves {{CLAIM:world_residual_finest}} across {{CLAIM:world_coverage_finest}} of it. No
resolution produces a residual larger than {{CLAIM:world_residual_max}} in absolute size.

Table 2 gives the ladder. Coverage falls as the strata get finer, since a stratum has to hold at
least 40 decisions in each arm before the two can be compared inside it, so the coarse resolutions
speak for the whole corpus and the finest for a little under half of it. The residual is small at
both ends and never approaches the raw gap anywhere in between. Coverage belongs beside every
residual for the same reason the rest of this paper exists — a rate whose denominator is not reported
reads as stronger than it is.

What the stratification leaves as the explanation is where each arm spends its run. The governed arm
takes {{CLAIM:tier0_gov_share}} of its decisions in shortage tier 0, the tier in which no shortage is
declared. The no-validator arm takes {{CLAIM:tier3_noval_share}} of its decisions in tier 3, severe
shortage. The two arms occupy different worlds, and within a world they report the same thing.

The agent is a faithful instrument. Same world in, same report out. This is a stronger result than
one in which the agent games the rule, since it needs no assumption of deception at all: a perfectly
honest agent breaks the conformance statistic on its own, and breaks it by roughly the amount the
intervention succeeded in moving the world. The metric is most misleading exactly when the
intervention is most effective.

Two further comparisons agree with the decomposition. At year 1 the two arms' worlds are identical —
exact string equality on all nine pre-decision columns over {{CLAIM:year1_matched_pairs}} matched
anchor pairs, with the first disagreement on any of them at year {{CLAIM:year1_divergence_year}} —
and the shift there is {{CLAIM:year1_high_delta}}, a null.
Retry pressure at year 1 is at its highest of the run. {{CLAIM:year1_retried_pct}} of governed year-1
decisions were rejected and re-prompted at least once, and {{CLAIM:year1_exhausted_pct}} of all
year-1 decisions ran to the end of the retry budget. Maximum pressure on an identical world moves the
reported appraisal by nothing measurable.

The second comparison runs the other way. On decisions the governance loop never spoke to — no
rejection, no re-prompt, no message of any kind — the shift is larger rather than smaller, in
{{CLAIM:neverretried_larger_cells}} irrigation cells: {{CLAIM:neverretried_delta_anchor}} in the
anchor cell against {{CLAIM:anchor_high_delta_bare}} over all of its decisions, and
{{CLAIM:pooled_neverretried_delta}} against {{CLAIM:pooled_alldecisions_delta}} when pooled over all
20 paired seeds. Selection on acceptance is not what produces the effect. The stratum is not
balanced across the two arms, and that caveat travels with the number — it covers
{{CLAIM:neverretried_share_gov}} of governed decisions against {{CLAIM:neverretried_share_noval}} of
no-validator ones, and the asymmetry is itself a consequence of enforcement.

What made the worlds differ is the reservoir. The governed arm ends {{CLAIM:reservoir_final_gap}}
higher on Lake Mead, {{CLAIM:reservoir_mean_gap}} averaged over the 42 simulated years. The
no-validator arm first touches the 895 ft floor in year {{CLAIM:noval_floor_years}} in all five
seeds, and the governed arm reaches that floor in none of them. The divergence is the governance
layer doing what it was installed to do, and it is also the entire source of the movement in the
statistic used to certify it.

One quantity stays out of reach. For a governed decision that was retried and then approved —
{{CLAIM:acceptance_selection_pct}} of governed decisions — the appraisal on record comes from the
accepted generation, so it is selected on the rule that judged it, and the first attempt is not
recoverable from this corpus (three separate checks, all negative, §8). The decomposition bounds that
concern rather than dismissing it. The never-retried stratum had no appraisal rewritten at all and
shows the effect more strongly, so whatever the retry loop does inside a decision, it is not what
produces the shift between arms.
