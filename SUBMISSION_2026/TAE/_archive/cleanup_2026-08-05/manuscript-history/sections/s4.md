The conformance statistic a behavioural norm reports is conditional. Its denominator is not every
decision but the decisions whose self-reported appraisal falls inside the rule's antecedent, and that
denominator is written by the same generation the rule governs. In the anchor cell the denominator
moves further than the rate it supports. The share of decisions reporting a high water-scarcity
appraisal is {{CLAIM:anchor_high_gov}}% with validators on against {{CLAIM:anchor_high_nov}}% with
them off, a difference of {{CLAIM:anchor_high_delta}}.

The rate conditioned on that appraisal moves less. It runs {{CLAIM:anchor_cond_gov}}% with validators
on against {{CLAIM:anchor_cond_nov}}% with them off, {{CLAIM:anchor_cond_delta}}. Two numbers
describe the same cell, one close to twice the size of the other, and only the smaller one is normally
reported.

Prevalence in both arms is the extra number. A conformance report gives the rate, sometimes the
denominator count, almost never the denominator's share of each arm's decisions side by side, and the
studies that produced this corpus did not report it either. Without it a reader cannot tell whether a
lower violation rate means agents violated a rule less often or means a smaller and differently
composed set of decisions entered the rule's scope at all.

Table 1 gives both quantities for every cell, on the reading and the inferential unit its caption
states.

Every irrigation cell moves the same way. The shift in high-appraisal prevalence runs from
{{CLAIM:irrigation_high_delta_range}} across the five cells, is positive in all of them, and reaches
*p* < 0.05 in each of the four cells with balanced seeds. The nine flood cells sit in a different
range entirely, {{CLAIM:flood_high_delta_range}} pp, mostly null, {{CLAIM:flood_positive_count}}
positive, one reaching significance (gemma4:26b, {{CLAIM:flood_sig_cell}}). The two groups separate
on enforcement as well. Governed rejection rates run from {{CLAIM:gov_rej_range_irrigation_perseed}}
across the irrigation cells against {{CLAIM:flood_pressure_range}} across the flood cells, where the
no-validator arm rejects {{CLAIM:flood_pressure_noval}}. No irrigation shift is smaller than any flood
shift, and no flood cell reaches the enforcement pressure of the least active irrigation cell.
Separation is complete on both axes.

Enforcement pressure is not an independent predictor here and must not be read as one. Regime is
perfectly confounded with testbed. All five high-enforcement cells are irrigation and all nine
low-enforcement cells are flood, so any apparent pressure-to-shift relation across the fourteen cells
is equally well described as a difference between two simulators. Within irrigation, where testbed is
held fixed, the relation does not run in the direction a pressure account needs. We report a regime
separation and claim no dose-response.

The near-null flood shifts show that the irrigation pattern is not an artefact of the appraisal
instrument or of one model family. They do not show what a low-enforcement irrigation would look
like, and no such cell exists in this corpus.

The confound costs the argument nothing, since the identifying evidence is in the next section and
lies entirely within irrigation, where §5 holds the world state fixed and compares the two arms
inside it at several resolutions. The fourteen cells are asked to establish one thing, that the
conditioning variable moves wherever enforcement is active, and the table does that on its own.
