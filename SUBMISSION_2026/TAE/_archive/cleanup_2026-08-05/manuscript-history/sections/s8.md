## 8 Limitations and what would settle it <!-- page budget: 0.5 pg -->

The appraisal on record for a governed decision that was retried and then approved comes from the
accepted generation, so it is selected on the rule that judged it. That stratum is
{{CLAIM:acceptance_selection_pct}} of governed decisions and its first attempt is not in the shipped
corpus, checked three ways, all negative. The never-retried stratum bounds it: no appraisal there was
rewritten at all, and the shift is larger, {{CLAIM:neverretried_delta_anchor}} against
{{CLAIM:anchor_high_delta_bare}} in the anchor cell and larger in
{{CLAIM:neverretried_larger_cells}} irrigation cells.

Stratification buys resolution with coverage. A stratum needs enough decisions in both arms, so the
finest strata reach {{CLAIM:world_coverage_finest}} of the anchor corpus, where the coarse strata
cover {{CLAIM:world_coverage_coarse}} and leave {{CLAIM:world_residual_coarse}}.

The corpus covers {{CLAIM:model_scope}} and no frontier models. The finding is architectural rather
than a capability limit, and a stronger model reports a changed world more accurately, which makes
the composition term larger rather than smaller. Enforcement regime is perfectly confounded with
testbed identity, so identification runs within irrigation and no cross-testbed contrast carries an
inference. One irrigation no-validator run was produced on a later code revision than the other
twenty and labels the same allocation-cap rejection differently in its audit; both labels map to
the precondition layer and no rate depends on the choice. Flood coverage of the per-run denominator runs
{{CLAIM:flood_coverage_range}}, by design rather than by loss.

Two missing arms would settle the rest. One logs the first-attempt appraisal before the retry loop
can rewrite it. The other runs enforcement on with the environment pinned to the untreated
trajectory. Neither exists here. This is one implementation on one governance stack, and the local
inference backend is not deterministic at a fixed seed, so every comparison is an N-seed aggregate.
