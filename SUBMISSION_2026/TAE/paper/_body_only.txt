# When the Guardrail Works, the Measurement Misleads: Conformance Rates for Agents That Report Their Own State






## Abstract 

Governance layers for language-model agents report success as a conformance rate. We show that
rate is least trustworthy on exactly the runs where the governance worked, and that no deceptive
agent is needed to make it so. Some of these rules condition on a state the agent itself reports
rather than on one the environment records. A norm of that kind has no observation channel independent of the process it governs, so
enforcement rewrites the distribution of its own antecedent and the reported gain cannot be separated
into behaviour and composition by that statistic. We measure the consequence on paired runs with
validators and without, over {{CLAIM:n_model_configs}} model configurations in two agent testbeds.
The conditioning variable moves further than the rate it supports, by
{{CLAIM:anchor_conj_delta_bare}} on the rule's two-part antecedent and
{{CLAIM:anchor_high_delta_bare}} on the scarcity conjunct the published statistics condition on,
in the anchor configuration, and the movement is composition of
world states rather than an agent working around the rule: holding the simulator-recorded world fixed
leaves {{CLAIM:world_residual_coarse}} at {{CLAIM:world_coverage_coarse}} coverage and
{{CLAIM:world_residual_finest}} at the finest stratification. An honest agent is enough to break the
statistic; no assumption of deception is needed. On one configuration the whole
apparent gain is composition, {{CLAIM:b12_cond_delta_bare}} conditional falling to
{{CLAIM:b12_uncond_delta}} over a denominator fixed by design, on three seeds. Moving the antecedent to world
state is tested here and fails. Report the conditioning variable's prevalence in both arms beside
every conformance rate, and log the world state a self-reported antecedent refers to.

## 1 Introduction 

Runtime governance layers for language-model agents are proliferating, and most share one shape. A
rule fires on a proposed action, the agent is told which rule it broke, and it is asked to try again.
AgentSpec [1], NeMo Guardrails [2], PolicyGuard [3] and SAVeR [4] all work this way, and all report
success as a conformance number, violations before enforcement against violations after. The shape is
older than the systems: normative multi-agent systems treated norm enforcement as a design problem in
its own right [5], and AMELI [6], OperA [7] and MOISE+ [8] each built an institution around it more
than two decades ago.

What a conformance number measures depends on what the norm reads. A rule can condition on state the
environment records, a budget or an allocation cap, or on state the agent itself reports in the
generation being judged, how severe it says a shortage is. We are not aware of an evaluation of the
second case, and it is the one this paper is about.

Enforcing over model-authored state is not new and we do not claim it. AgentSpec's rule language [1]
places no constraint on what a predicate may read, so a rule firing on a self-reported appraisal is
well formed in it today, and SAVeR [4] (Self-Audited Verified Reasoning) already checks an agent's
internal belief states before an action commits. What follows is an evaluation result, not a
mechanism, and the property that sets the self-reported case apart is not the obvious one.

It is not endogeneity. A predicate over program state after an agent has
edited files is treatment-affected as well; Gao et al. [9] name post-treatment risk-set bias in agentic
code repair, the classical problem of adjusting for a treatment-affected variable [10], and offer a
remedy, branching from frozen common states, available because their conditioning variable is
external to the generation. A world predicate has an observation channel independent of what the
agent writes, since the state can be frozen and re-entered. A self-reported predicate has only the
generation being governed. Enforcement rewrites the distribution of its own antecedent and cannot be
made not to, since freezing the appraisal destroys the thing being measured.

This paper reports four results. The conditioning variable moves further than the conformance rate it
supports (§4). The movement is composition of world states rather than an agent working around the
rule, measured in the anchor configuration by holding the simulator's own record of the world
fixed (§5). On one configuration
the whole apparent gain is composition (§6). The obvious repair, moving the antecedent to world
state, is tested here and fails (§7).

## 2 Setup 

Both testbeds run the same governed loop. The agent returns an action and a self-reported appraisal of
its situation in one generation, a validator stack screens the proposal against that appraisal and
against the state the simulator recorded, and an ERROR-level violation returns the broken rule and
re-prompts, up to {{CLAIM:retry_budget}} attempts, after which a domain default executes. The rule
under study forbids an increase in demand when the reported water scarcity is high and the reported
coping capacity is also high. That two-part condition is the rule's antecedent, what has to hold
before the rule applies at all, and both halves of it are labels the agent writes in the generation
being judged. The primary quantity below is the scarcity half alone, a superset of the antecedent and
the one the published statistics condition on. §4 puts the coping half and the antecedent itself
beside it.

Irrigation is the primary testbed: 78 farm agents over 42 years, {{CLAIM:decisions_per_run}} decisions
per run, a dual-appraisal theory of scarcity and coping, {{CLAIM:irrigation_action_count}} actions,
and a water right capping what any agent may withdraw. A
reservoir is coupled to those withdrawals, so an action in one year changes the state the next year's
decision sees. Flood is the second testbed, Protection Motivation Theory [11] over
{{CLAIM:flood_action_count}} actions on the same loop, a portability demonstration with no
action-to-environment feedback and no standing as a control for enforcement pressure.

The corpus holds {{CLAIM:n_model_configs}} model configurations from {{CLAIM:model_scope}}, each run
with validators on and off, all nine in flood and five in irrigation, giving fourteen
model × testbed cells. The two arms, validators on and validators off, are paired within a cell,
{{CLAIM:n_seeds}} seeds in most and fewer in two irrigation cells, with *n* in Table 1.

One decision shows what the published statistic counts. An agent reports high water scarcity and high
coping capacity, and proposes an increase in demand. That combination is what the rule forbids, so
the proposal is rejected, the agent is told which rule it broke, and it answers again. The statistic
then asks, among the decisions where the agent reported high scarcity, how many still asked for more
water. In the anchor cell, gemma3:4b on irrigation and the one §5 measures the mechanism on, the
answer is {{CLAIM:anchor_cond_gov}}% with validators on and {{CLAIM:anchor_cond_nov}}% with them off.
The set those two rates run over is not fixed. Decisions reporting high scarcity are
{{CLAIM:anchor_high_gov}}% of all decisions with validators on and {{CLAIM:anchor_high_nov}}% with
them off. §4 measures the gap between those two populations and §5 asks what opened it.

## 3 The two rule layers separate 

Only part of the stack reads what the agent says, and the paper needs that part isolated.
World-feasibility rules read
state that the simulator records and the agent does not author, such as a water-right allocation cap,
an aggregate demand ceiling, or a curtailment level. Theory-coherence rules read the agent's own
reported appraisal, the scarcity and coping labels written in the same generation that chooses the
action. A distinction of this kind is presentational unless the layers behave differently in the data,
so we measure it first.

At the layer level the corpus separates them cleanly. In the anchor cell (five seeds,
{{CLAIM:anchor_arm_n}} decisions per arm) the validators-off arm rejects {{CLAIM:novrej_pct}}
of decisions, and all {{CLAIM:novrej_count}} are a bare precondition failure with no validator rule
evaluated. The validators-on arm rejects {{CLAIM:govrej_pct}}, and all {{CLAIM:govrej_count}} of those
name a validator rule, none of them a bare precondition (Figure 2a). The split is exact in
{{CLAIM:rule_purity}} irrigation model-arm cells (Figure 2b), over governed rejection rates spanning
{{CLAIM:govrej_range}} and validators-off rates spanning {{CLAIM:novrej_range}}, both pooled across
seeds.

Layer-level separation does not make the governed rejections behavioural theory. The validator stack
carries rules of both kinds and the
rejections divide accordingly: {{CLAIM:appraisal_conditioned_pct}} of the {{CLAIM:govrej_count}} name
at least one rule whose antecedent is the reported appraisal, and the remaining
{{CLAIM:world_conditioned_pct}} name only world-conditioned rules. Every claim that follows concerns
the appraisal-conditioned subset, a minority of the enforcement a reader would see summarised as one
rejection rate, and the arm it is compared against is not unconstrained either.

The water-right precondition still binds without validators, and it binds on exactly the action the
conformance statistic counts. It is attached to the two
increase skills and to no other skill in the registry, so the {{CLAIM:novrej_increase_pct}} of
validators-off rejections that are increase proposals (Figure 2c) follows from the configuration
rather than from behaviour. In the anchor cell the constraint does real work,
with {{CLAIM:atcap_noval_pct}} of validators-off agent-years sitting at or above 99% of the water
right against {{CLAIM:atcap_gov_pct}} with validators on. The other four configurations are not like
this, with validators-off rejection rates from {{CLAIM:novrej_range_excl_anchor}}, so any statement
that both arms reject a large share holds for the anchor cell and nowhere else.

The two layers also do not block the same actions. Across the five irrigation configurations,
{{CLAIM:govrej_increase_pct}} of validators-on rejections fall on increase proposals and
{{CLAIM:govrej_decrease_pct}} on decrease proposals, which the world layer has no precondition for at
all. The appraisal-conditioned layer is measurable, and it is the only part of the stack whose
antecedent the agent writes. The rest of the paper is about that part.

## 4 The conditioning variable moves 



The conditioning variable moves further than the rate it supports. In the anchor cell that is
{{CLAIM:anchor_high_delta}} on prevalence against {{CLAIM:anchor_cond_delta}} on the rate. Only the
smaller is normally reported. Without the larger one a lower violation rate cannot
be told from a smaller and differently composed set of decisions entering the rule's scope.

Coping moves too, so neither half describes the antecedent on its own. It is reported high in
{{CLAIM:anchor_cope_gov}}% of governed anchor decisions against {{CLAIM:anchor_cope_nov}}% without
validators, {{CLAIM:anchor_cope_delta}}, and the conjunction the rule fires on runs
{{CLAIM:anchor_conj_gov}}% against {{CLAIM:anchor_conj_nov}}%, {{CLAIM:anchor_conj_delta}}, a
conditional gap of {{CLAIM:anchor_conj_rate_gap}}. A second enforced rule, on a very low reported
scarcity over a disjoint set, moves {{CLAIM:anchor_r2_delta}}.

The anchor cell is not special. Every irrigation cell moves the same way,
{{CLAIM:irrigation_high_delta_range}} across the five,
positive in all and *p* < 0.05 in each of the four with balanced seeds. The nine flood cells sit
elsewhere, {{CLAIM:flood_high_delta_range}} pp, mostly null,
{{CLAIM:flood_positive_count}} positive, one significant (gemma4:26b, {{CLAIM:flood_sig_cell}}), and
enforcement is as far apart, {{CLAIM:gov_rej_range_irrigation_perseed}} of governed irrigation
decisions rejected on Table 1's per-seed-mean basis against {{CLAIM:flood_pressure_range}} in flood,
where the no-validator arm rejects {{CLAIM:flood_pressure_noval}}. We report a regime separation and
claim no dose-response. Regime is perfectly confounded with testbed, so any pressure-to-shift relation
across the fourteen cells describes two simulators as well as it describes pressure, and within
irrigation it runs against what a pressure account needs. The near-null flood shifts show only that
the irrigation pattern is not an artefact of the appraisal instrument or of one model family.
Identification is in §5.

## 5 Why it moves — the mechanism 

In the anchor cell the shift in the conditioning variable is composition of world states, not a
change in what the agent says about a given world. The world the agent is reporting on — shortage
tier, reservoir elevation, drought index — is written by the simulator, not by the generation under
judgement, so it can be held fixed after the fact. That independence is from the generation and not
from the treatment, which moved the reservoir and with it the distribution of the world state; §7
turns on the second. Standardising both arms on a common distribution of that world state, so that
like world is compared with like, leaves a residual near zero at every resolution the data supports.
Against a raw gap in
HIGH-appraisal prevalence of
{{CLAIM:anchor_high_delta}}, holding shortage tier fixed leaves {{CLAIM:world_residual_coarse}}
across {{CLAIM:world_coverage_coarse}} of the anchor corpus, and the finest stratification leaves
{{CLAIM:world_residual_finest}} across {{CLAIM:world_coverage_finest}} of it. No resolution produces
a residual larger than {{CLAIM:world_residual_max}}.

In the anchor cell this is a stronger result than
one in which the agent games the rule, since it needs no assumption of deception at all: a perfectly
honest agent breaks the conformance statistic on its own, and does so because the enforcement moved

## 6 What it does to the statistic 



Recomputed over a denominator the design fixes in advance — 78 agents by 42 years,
{{CLAIM:decisions_per_run}} decisions per run in both arms, none of it authored by the model — the
reported effect shrinks in every irrigation cell and on one of them disappears (Figure 4).

On the anchor cell the effect survives de-conditioning almost intact,
{{CLAIM:anchor_cond_delta_bare}} conditional against {{CLAIM:anchor_uncond_delta_bare}}
unconditional, a gap of {{CLAIM:anchor_gap}}. The two gemma4 cells widen from there, gemma4:e2b from
{{CLAIM:e2b_cond_delta}} to {{CLAIM:e2b_uncond_delta}} and gemma4:e4b from
{{CLAIM:e4b_cond_delta}} to {{CLAIM:e4b_uncond_delta}}. gemma3:12b goes the whole way.

On that cell the entire apparent effect is composition, {{CLAIM:b12_cond_delta}} becoming
{{CLAIM:b12_uncond_delta}}. The conditioning variable's distribution moved and the increase rate over
a fixed denominator did not, so a reader given only the conditional number would credit the
governance layer with a behaviour change this corpus does not show.

One cell stands outside all of this. ministral3:8b's arms are unbalanced,
{{CLAIM:ministral_balance}}, so a paired test over seeds has nothing to pair, and it is excluded from
every paired interval and significance test. The
exclusion follows a design criterion applied in code before these rates were computed, and the cell is
drawn without a confidence interval rather than dropped, so its conditional
{{CLAIM:ministral_cond_delta}} and unconditional {{CLAIM:ministral_uncond_delta}} stay on the page,
the sign reversal on that cell uninterpreted.

The unconditional rate is not a corrected version of the conditional one. It answers how often the
agent asked for more water out of every decision it made, not out of the decisions where it reported
high scarcity. What survives de-conditioning on the anchor is a quantity of similar size, not the
same quantity.

Both rates use the LAST-PROPOSED action reading, the one the published statistics use. Substituting
the executed action on rejected rows moves the gemma3:12b unconditional delta to
{{CLAIM:b12_uncond_exec_delta}}, so the near-zero result on that cell belongs to the published reading
and has to be reported as belonging to it.

A denominator fixed by design also admits an external reference the conditional rate cannot use. The
irrigation action set holds {{CLAIM:action_set_shape}}, so a uniformly random agent would propose an
increase on {{CLAIM:uniform_null}} of its decisions. Without validators the unconditional rate sits
far above that null ({{CLAIM:anchor_uncond_nov}}, {{CLAIM:nov_null_t}}, 95% CI
{{CLAIM:nov_null_ci}}). With validators it cannot be distinguished from it
({{CLAIM:anchor_uncond_gov}}, {{CLAIM:gov_null_t}}, 95% CI {{CLAIM:gov_null_ci}}). No such comparison
exists for the conditional rate, whose denominator is written by the process under evaluation. The
null is weak and bounds the direction only: a marginal rate at chance means no net pull toward
increases, not that governed behaviour is random.

## 7 What to do instead 

The obvious repair fails, and we tested it on the anchor cell. Condition the norm on world state
instead of on what the agent says, and the antecedent moves as much or more: shortage tier 2 or worse
shifts {{CLAIM:world_tier2_shift}} against the self-reported conjunct's
{{CLAIM:anchor_high_delta_bare}}, tier 1 or worse and a nonzero curtailment ratio each shift
{{CLAIM:world_tier1_curtail_shift}}, and a drought index at or above 0.5 shifts
{{CLAIM:world_drought_shift}}. The world state is not written by the generation, which is what let §5
hold it fixed, and it is still downstream of the treatment. Nothing in a coupled system sits upstream
of the intervention, so the repair buys a differently observed conditioning variable rather than a
steadier one. It is the first fix a reader proposes, and it now has a measurement behind it.

Three things survive, and a practitioner can do all of them tomorrow. Report the conditioning
variable's prevalence in both arms beside every conformance rate. It is one extra number, almost never
reported, and the studies that produced this corpus did not report it either.

If a norm reads a state the agent reports, log the independent world state that report is about.
Without it the number cannot be decomposed at all. §5's stratification ran only because the simulator
happened to write shortage tier, reservoir elevation and drought index to a record the generation
never touches. A deployment that logs rule fires, retries and outcomes and nothing in that role
carries the same shift with no way to find it.

Where a design fixes a denominator, report the rate over it beside the conditional one, and say
plainly that it is a different estimand rather than a corrected effect size. The two answer different
questions, and on one configuration they disagree completely.

The point outlasts this testbed. Where the antecedent of a rule is written by the process the rule
governs, the only available control is to detect a violation after the fact, and detection of that
kind moves the thing it detects.

## 8 Limitations and what would settle it 

The appraisal on record for a governed decision that was retried and then approved comes from the
accepted generation, so it is selected on the rule that judged it. That stratum is
{{CLAIM:acceptance_selection_pct}} of governed decisions and its first attempt is not in the shipped
corpus, checked three ways, all negative. The never-retried stratum bounds it: no appraisal there was
rewritten, and the shift is larger, {{CLAIM:neverretried_delta_anchor}} against
{{CLAIM:anchor_high_delta_bare}} in the anchor cell and in {{CLAIM:neverretried_larger_cells}}
irrigation cells.

Stratification buys resolution with coverage, the finest strata reaching
{{CLAIM:world_coverage_finest}} of the anchor corpus against {{CLAIM:world_coverage_coarse}} at the
coarse ones. The corpus covers {{CLAIM:model_scope}} and no frontier models, and both size endpoints are
flood-only. Whether the finding is architectural rather than a capability limit is untested here.
Regime is perfectly confounded with testbed, so identification runs within irrigation and no
cross-testbed contrast carries an inference. One irrigation configuration is unbalanced,
{{CLAIM:ministral_balance}}, excluded from every paired interval and test. Flood carries three
provenance defects: audit coverage of the per-run denominator runs {{CLAIM:flood_coverage_range}}, by
design rather than by loss; manifest seeds disagree across arms in
{{CLAIM:flood_manifest_disagree}}, where pairing is by run index; and one governed gemma3:4b run used
a shorter prompt than the other four in its cell. None bears on irrigation.

Two missing arms would settle the rest: one logging the first-attempt appraisal before the retry loop
can rewrite it, the other running enforcement with the environment pinned to the untreated
trajectory. Neither exists here. This is one implementation on one governance stack, and the
inference backend is not deterministic at a fixed seed, so every comparison is an N-seed aggregate.
