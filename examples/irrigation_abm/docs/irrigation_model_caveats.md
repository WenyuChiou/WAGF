# Irrigation model caveats (2026-09-16 review)

The model is a stated simplification of Hung & Yang (2021) RL-ABM-CRSS, not a reproduction.

## Methods/SI Caveats

1. "Lake Powell and Lake Mead are two annual storage stocks in a closed-form surrogate water balance, not RiverWare/CRSS as in Hung & Yang (2021). Natural flow at Lees Ferry is the USBR calendar-year naturalized flow of the historical year Hung and Yang's own resample maps to (2019-2046 from 1988-2015, 2047-2060 from 1934-1947), not derived from precipitation, and the same 42-row trace drives every arm and seed. Upper Basin non-farm consumptive use (min(NF, 1.2213) MAF/yr, USBR 2016-2020 Consumptive Uses and Losses) is removed from natural flow before Upper Basin agriculture and Lake Powell, held constant and never curtailed. The Upper Basin has PRIORITY and is never cut to protect Powell's release (PI decision 2026-09-28): it is bounded only by the 5.0 MAF delivery capacity and the non-farm-net natural flow itself; a NEW gap code, `ub_natural_flow`, names which of the two actually bound. Lake Powell releases R, planned once (in `advance_year`, before agents decide) from that year's PROJECTED end-of-year contents for BOTH basins (re-review 2026-09-29, HIGH 1 froze the Lower Basin projection; HIGH A, same day round 3, moved the whole planning step into its own method and froze the Upper Basin projection too, after the reviewer found the release still moved up to 0.237 MAF in 16 of 42 years when only the Lower Basin side was frozen), chosen to equalize Powell's and Mead's end-of-year LIVE-storage fractions AFTER that year's projected Lower Basin withdrawals (a simplification of the 2007 Interim Guidelines' balancing tiers, ROD Section 6; PI decision 2026-09-28, superseding a same-day pass-through-with-target-fill rule, then re-reviewed 2026-09-29 to include the withdrawal term the first version omitted), bounded to the Guidelines' own Lower Elevation Balancing Tier band [7.0, 9.5] MAF and never more than physically available (pass-through at dead pool, applied in `_settle` from THAT call's own actual inflow/storage, which may legitimately differ from the plan). Lake Mead's storage-elevation relation is the USBR 2009 area-capacity survey (`data/mead_capacity_usbr2009_1ft.csv`); storage above the conservation-pool top (28.647 MAF total, not the curve's 30.167 MAF flood-control endpoint) is treated as spill. Not the full operating rules of the 2007 Interim Guidelines (the four named tiers collapse into one balancing rule; no Lake Mead protection levels). Diversions are treated as fully consumptive (no return flow)." (v3 water-system rewrite and same-day review round, 2026-09-28/29, `irrigation_v3_spec_2026-09-28.md` WP1, closing L2 H1, H2, H4, D1, D5, D6; before it, natural flow was `12.0 x (precipitation / 100)` clamped to [6, 17] MAF, the Upper Basin was cut pro-rata whenever Powell's storage could not sustain a 7.0 MAF release, and the Mead curve was a 10-point approximation that did not match the cited USBR table. The pre-v3 fixed-2018-demand trajectory reported here before this rewrite -- Tier 2/3 in 6 of 42 years, mean 1 January Mead 1081 ft -- no longer describes the current physics; see the WP1 implementation's own verification output for the current trajectory. Supply-side redesign, 2026-09-25, before which the balance tracked Lake Mead only with a +/-3.5 MAF clamp on its annual storage change, still applies structurally: two storage stocks, one idempotent settlement per year from a frozen 1 January snapshot.)
2. "The declared shortage tier (1 January Lake Mead elevation per the 2007 Colorado River Interim Guidelines, Section 2.D.1: 0 / 0.333 / 0.417 / 0.500 MAF by tier) is borne ENTIRELY by the 5.0 MAF Lower Basin non-agricultural bucket -- the model's stand-in for CAP and Southern Nevada, the Guidelines' actual juniors (ROD Section 2.D, p. 36-37: shortages reduce Arizona and Nevada, California stays at 4.4 MAF; Colorado River Compact Decree Art. II(B)(3) priority order). The 22 Lower Basin agricultural agents are modelled as senior mainstem or California entitlement holders and are NOT reduced by the declared tier -- a simplification: in reality a few Arizona fourth-priority mainstem contracts share shortages with CAP, and this model treats all 22 as senior instead of separating them out. Agricultural agents are reduced only by physical Mead availability (`lb_mead_availability`), the same mechanism that can also reduce the non-agricultural bucket and Mexico's delivery. The declared tier stays visible to agents as information (`shortage_tier`, `curtailment_ratio` = declared volume / non-agricultural bucket) without implying it reduced their own delivery. Upper Basin agents are limited by the 5.0 MAF delivery capacity and the non-farm-net natural flow only -- never by Lake Powell's own release needs (see item 1)." (PI decision 2026-09-28, superseding a same-day WP1 version that shared the declared volume pro rata across ALL Lower Basin use including agricultural requests, and before that the pre-v3 flat 0/5/10/20 percent cut, `irrigation_v3_spec_2026-09-28.md` WP1, closing L2 D2. Consequence for governance counts: `curtailment_awareness_check` and `supply_gap_block_increase` read `curtailment_ratio`, which is now 0 for every Upper Basin agent and nonzero for every Lower Basin agent exactly when a tier is declared (unchanged from the pre-review behaviour, even though the number itself no longer represents a ratio applied to agricultural deliveries).)
3. "Superseded 2026-09-25: the demand-ceiling (6.0 MAF basin request) and demand-floor (50% of water right) stabilizers are retired from dispatch; the basin limit is physical availability in the water balance, and the basin request / 5.86 MAF CRSS target ratio is logged as `basin_request_over_target`." Corpora produced before that date ran both rules; for them the original caveat applies: "the corridors are framework-added stabilization heuristics, not institutional rules from Hung & Yang (2021); the 6.0 MAF ceiling is checked against the previous year's aggregate demand, not a live intra-year total."
4. "The 70% fulfilment cutoff used by `supply_gap_block_increase` is an unvalidated modeling choice, not derived from CRSS or the Interim Guidelines."
5. "Calibrated FQL parameters (mu, sigma, alpha, regret) from Hung & Yang (2021) assign each of the 78 agents to one of three behavioral clusters via nearest-centroid classification (a persona label only), and mu and sigma also set each agent's step size exactly as in Hung & Yang: step = (mu + |N(0, sigma)|) x bin_size, with bin_size the step of the agent's discretised diversion grid; the LLM chooses the direction and whether the step is small (at or below the agent's median step) or large (above it). Cluster centroids were not rebalanced to a target distribution for the reported run; the realized split (67 aggressive / 6 myopic-conservative / 5 forward-looking-conservative of 78) should not be assumed to match the paper's own cluster proportions." (Before 2026-09-26 the step was a persona-scaled percentage drawn from fixed 1-8 / 8-20 percent bands.)
6. "Within each Upper Basin state group, individual agents share identical calibrated behavioral parameters (only water rights and narrative persona text differ), reflecting the state-group resolution at which the source calibration was performed."
7. RESOLVED 2026-09-28 (v3, `irrigation_v3_spec_2026-09-28.md` WP1, closing L1-03 and the environment side of L3 M9): each Upper Basin agent now reads its OWN sub-basin's winter precipitation column (WY, UT1-3, NM, CO1-3, AZ; AZ_UB agents read "AZ"), carried on the profile as `precip_group` and exposed per agent by `IrrigationEnvironment.get_agent_context`. A profile with no `precip_group` (synthetic/mock) falls back to the pre-v3 basin-wide mean, which otherwise survives only as the (unread) legacy `drought_index` input. Superseded text: "Precipitation is generated as a single Upper-Basin-wide signal shared by all Upper Basin agents, rather than per-state-group signals as in the reference model." The link between this signal and natural flow (item 1) remains weak in the underlying data (same-year correlation -0.17 to 0.26 across columns; Hung, Son and Yang 2022 call it "winter precipitation forecast information in the next year") -- disclosed, not fixed, because the model does not use precipitation to drive natural flow at all (item 1).
8. "In the `strict` governance profile, drought-perception rules (WSA/ACA labels) constrain the LLM's own self-reported scarcity labels and are not cross-checked against the environment's computed drought index; only the `grounded_v1` profile performs that grounding."
9. "A cold-start grace period converts Tier-2 shortage blocks into warnings for simulation years 1-3, making governance strictness non-uniform across the study horizon within the `strict` profile."
10. "Per-agent logged diversion is the delivered water from the year's final settlement (after every decision), so the per-agent column sums to the basin deliveries used in the water balance." (Before the F11b and supply-side fixes, Upper Basin rows could carry the pre-Powell-clamp value.)
11. "Differences from the Hung & Yang (2021) action rule: WAGF applies the step (mu + |N(0, sigma)|) x bin_size to the agent's REQUEST (H&Y: to last year's diversion), floors a decrease at min(10 percent of the water right, current request) (H&Y: at zero) and caps an increase at the water right (H&Y apply no cap inside Q_learning). The LLM chooses the direction and a small (at or below the agent's median step) or large step instead of H&Y's epsilon-greedy choice between the two directions." (Water-right history-anchor, 2026-09-26: for a real 78-agent run the water right is no longer always the top of the H&Y grid -- it is min(grid_top, (1 + paper_water_margin) x observed_peak), paper_water_margin default 0.4; see `docs/irrigation_physics.md` (F10 note) for the derivation and the F04-style finding that motivated it: at the bare grid top, Upper Basin requests grew to 7-10 MAF within a few years and Lake Powell sat at dead pool for roughly half the run. The FQL baseline (`run_fql_baseline.py`, `create_fql_agents`) keeps its own Q-grid (state discretisation and the regret-step scale) on the ORIGINAL H&Y grid top (`grid_top_af`), not the capped `water_right`. The capped water_right reaches the FQL only through the environment and validator context shared with the LLM path: the increase clip, the 10 percent decrease floor, and the allocation-cap and minimum-use flags the validators read; the FQL Q-grid stays on the Hung and Yang grid top. Building the Q-grid itself from the capped water_right would have shrunk the FQL's internal bins and regret step for every peak-scaled agent, changing the FQL reference model beyond the intended request clip.)
13. "The FQL calibrated warm-start (`--fql-init calibrated`, default since 2026-09-27) loads each agent's Q_Table/count/P_Tran from `ref/CRSS_DB/CRSS_DB/Initial_Files/All-1109`, one of six undocumented snapshots shipped in the reference bundle (All, All-0913, All-1020, All-1022, All-1109, All-WRR) with no README, note, or code comment stating which is canonical. All-1109 was chosen because the per-agent FQL parameters loaded from `ALL_colorado_ABM_params_cal_1108.csv` are dated 2020-11-08, and All-1109 is the only snapshot whose Q tables are ALSO dated 2020-11-08 (all 78 files) -- i.e. the only one where the loaded parameters and the loaded learned state come from the same calibration run. `All` (Q tables dated 2021-05-28, seven months later, with no matching parameter file shipped in this release) is what the authors' own `initialization_ABM_CRSS_v3.py` copies by default; `All-WRR` is a composite of the 2020-09-13 Upper Basin and 2020-10-20 Lower Basin batches. This choice is to be confirmed with the model's authors; the other five snapshots are reported as a sensitivity in the SI, not silently dropped. The chosen directory name and its Q-table mtime date range are recorded in `reproducibility_manifest.json` for every calibrated run."
14. "Memory retrieval follows the SI section 3.8 composition from 2026-09-26: the 5 most recent memories plus up to 2 long-term memories with the highest decayed importance, up to 7 per prompt. Corpora produced before that served 3 memories per prompt, chosen by weighted recency / importance / context scoring (the provider's hard-coded top_k=3); the SI text did not describe those runs. Two other reads changed with it: the year-end reflection input (was up to 10 items by weighted score, now the same up to 7 by recency and decayed importance) and the simulation_log memory column (was up to 5, now up to 7)." Details the SI text should state: a memory is long-term (eligible for a significant slot) only if consolidated, which requires importance >= 0.6 and then happens with probability 0.7 x importance; decay age counts stored memory items, not years (a consolidated item advances the item clock by 2); decayed importance at or below 0.05 drops an item; ties keep the earlier item. Candidates that duplicate a window item's text are skipped, and the candidate pool is bounded (top_k_significant + window + 2), so with many duplicates a significant slot can stay empty.
