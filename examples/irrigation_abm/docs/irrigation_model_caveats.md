# Irrigation model caveats (2026-09-16 review)

The model is a stated simplification of Hung & Yang (2021) RL-ABM-CRSS, not a reproduction.

## Methods/SI Caveats

1. "Lake Powell and Lake Mead are two annual storage stocks in a closed-form surrogate water balance, not RiverWare/CRSS as in Hung & Yang (2021). Natural flow at Lees Ferry is the USBR calendar-year naturalized flow of the historical year Hung and Yang's own resample maps to (2019-2046 from 1988-2015, 2047-2060 from 1934-1947), not derived from precipitation, and the same 42-row trace drives every arm and seed. Upper Basin non-farm consumptive use (min(NF, 1.2213) MAF/yr, USBR 2016-2020 Consumptive Uses and Losses) is removed from natural flow before Upper Basin agriculture and Lake Powell, held constant and never curtailed. The Upper Basin has PRIORITY and is never cut to protect Powell's release (PI decision 2026-09-28): it is bounded only by the 5.0 MAF delivery capacity and the non-farm-net natural flow itself; a NEW gap code, `ub_natural_flow`, names which of the two actually bound. Lake Powell releases R, planned once (in `advance_year`, before agents decide) from that year's PROJECTED end-of-year contents for BOTH basins (re-review 2026-09-29, HIGH 1 froze the Lower Basin projection; HIGH A, same day round 3, moved the whole planning step into its own method and froze the Upper Basin projection too, after the reviewer found the release still moved up to 0.237 MAF in 16 of 42 years when only the Lower Basin side was frozen), by EITHER of two rules (`WaterSystemConfig.powell_release_mode`, items 26-27, 2026-09-29): the ACTUAL 2007 Interim Guidelines Equalization / Upper Elevation Balancing / Mid-Elevation Release / Lower Elevation Balancing tiers, chosen from the 1 January storages (`"ig2007_tiers"`, the v3 default -- see caveat 17; ONLY its Lower Elevation Balancing tier, and the April escalations that happen to land inside that band, are bounded to [7.0, 9.5] MAF -- the Equalization tier's own release has NO upper bound and can exceed it, e.g. 14.63 MAF in the 2011 hindcast year, 13.95 MAF in a 2028 replay-A year, 5 of 42 replay-A years above 9.5 MAF in total), or a one-rule simplification equalizing Powell's and Mead's end-of-year LIVE-storage fractions after that year's projected Lower Basin withdrawals, bounded to the Guidelines' own Lower Elevation Balancing Tier band [7.0, 9.5] MAF ALWAYS (`"storage_balancing"`, PI decision 2026-09-28, now the SI mode). Either way R is never more than physically available (pass-through at dead pool, applied in `_settle` from THAT call's own actual inflow/storage, which may legitimately differ from the plan). Lake Mead's storage-elevation relation is the USBR 2009 area-capacity survey (`data/mead_capacity_usbr2009_1ft.csv`); storage above the conservation-pool top (28.647 MAF total, not the curve's 30.167 MAF flood-control endpoint) is treated as spill. Diversions are treated as fully consumptive (no return flow)." (v3 water-system rewrite and same-day review round, 2026-09-28/29, `irrigation_v3_spec_2026-09-28.md` WP1, closing L2 H1, H2, H4, D1, D5, D6; before it, natural flow was `12.0 x (precipitation / 100)` clamped to [6, 17] MAF, the Upper Basin was cut pro-rata whenever Powell's storage could not sustain a 7.0 MAF release, and the Mead curve was a 10-point approximation that did not match the cited USBR table. The pre-v3 fixed-2018-demand trajectory reported here before this rewrite -- Tier 2/3 in 6 of 42 years, mean 1 January Mead 1081 ft -- no longer describes the current physics; see the WP1 implementation's own verification output for the current trajectory. Supply-side redesign, 2026-09-25, before which the balance tracked Lake Mead only with a +/-3.5 MAF clamp on its annual storage change, still applies structurally: two storage stocks, one idempotent settlement per year from a frozen 1 January snapshot.)
